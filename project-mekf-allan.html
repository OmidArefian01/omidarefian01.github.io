Project Overview

The filter is only as credible as its uncertainty model

State estimation is not only the calculation of a smooth trajectory. The covariance must describe what the estimator knows, what it does not know, and how strongly each sensor should influence the state under real flight conditions.

The engineering problem

GPS-denied navigation with sensors that change behavior

The drone predicts motion from the IMU and corrects it with barometric height, downward range, optical-flow velocity, and magnetic heading. Noise measured while the vehicle is motionless is often much smaller than the effective error seen in flight, where vibration, tilt, height, timing, and model mismatch also contribute.

If \(Q\) is too small, the filter becomes overconfident in propagation. If \(R\) is too small, measurements fail innovation gates or pull the state aggressively. Increasing either matrix without physical evidence can hide a modelling error instead of fixing it.

The contribution

One traceable evidence chain

  • Characterize stationary accelerometer and gyroscope noise across time scales.
  • Use Allan, Hadamard, Welch PSD, residual distributions, and datasheets as complementary evidence.
  • Convert only defensible coefficients into the continuous process-noise matrix \(Q_c\).
  • Estimate measurement-noise floors, then validate effective \(R\) values through complete-flight MEKF replay.
  • Judge the result using NIS, rejection rate, covariance health, trajectory behavior, and no-GPS EKF2 agreement.
⚠

Important: PX4 EKF2 is used only after each independent MEKF replay for one constant frame alignment and comparison. Because both estimators share sensors, agreement is not independent ground-truth accuracy.

Evidence Architecture

No single test is allowed to identify every covariance

Fast IMU noise, slow bias evolution, static sensor scatter, and dynamic measurement error are different physical quantities. The workflow separates them before combining them in the MEKF.

From recordings to a filter that can be audited

Every accepted parameter retains its source and confidence level. Measured white-noise densities are separated from provisional bias random walks, while dynamic replay prevents static lower bounds from being used blindly in flight.

Allan and Hadamard Analysis

Measure how sensor stability changes with averaging time

Ordinary variance returns one number for one dataset. Allan and Hadamard deviation return curves, revealing which processes dominate at short, medium, and long averaging times.

Overlapping Allan deviation

Difference between adjacent cluster averages

For averaging time \(\tau\), samples are grouped into local averages \(\bar y_i(\tau)\). Allan variance measures how successive averages change:

\[\sigma_A^2(\tau)=\frac{1}{2(M-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\left(\bar y_{i+1}-\bar y_i\right)^2.\]

A log–log slope near \(-1/2\) indicates white noise. A flat region suggests bias-instability behavior. A sustained \(+1/2\) region can support a random-walk coefficient, but only when enough independent clusters exist.

Overlapping Hadamard deviation

Second difference suppresses constant linear drift

Hadamard variance compares three adjacent cluster averages:

\[\sigma_H^2(\tau)=\frac{1}{6(M-2)}\sum_{i=1}^{M-2}\left(\bar y_{i+2}-2\bar y_{i+1}+\bar y_i\right)^2.\]

If Allan deviation rises strongly at large \(\tau\) while Hadamard deviation stays lower, deterministic linear drift is a likely contributor. If both rise similarly, a stochastic slow process is more plausible, although temperature and environment must still be checked.

Process Noise Identification

Building \(Q_c\) without pretending every term was measured

Process noise describes uncertainty entering through IMU propagation and bias evolution. The latest conservative recommendation combines measured short-term densities with clearly labelled provisional random walks.

Continuous process-noise model

Evidence-based and provisional blocks remain visibly separate

\[Q_c=\operatorname{diag}\!\left((1.2\!\times\!10^{-3})^2I_3, (6.8\!\times\!10^{-5})^2I_3, (3.0\!\times\!10^{-3})^2I_3, (3.0\!\times\!10^{-4})^2I_3, \sigma_{bm}^2I_3, (1.0\!\times\!10^{-2})^2\right).\]

The first two blocks represent accelerometer and gyroscope white-noise densities. The next blocks describe accelerometer-, gyroscope-, magnetometer-, and barometer-bias evolution. The magnetic-bias term remains symbolic because the required long raw-field dataset has not yet been collected.

Measurement Noise Identification

Static scatter is a lower bound; flight replay decides operational \(R\)

Measurement covariance includes more than electronic noise. In flight it also absorbs vibration, geometry, timing, tilt compensation, surface texture, lighting, and measurement-model mismatch.

Measurement update

Innovation covariance and scalar consistency

\[\nu_k=z_k-h(\hat x_k^-),\qquad S_k=H_kP_k^-H_k^T+R_k.\]
\[\operatorname{NIS}_k=\frac{\nu_k^2}{S_k}.\]

For a statistically consistent scalar update, mean NIS should be near one and approximately 95% of samples should lie below 3.841. A high value can result from underestimated \(R\), underestimated \(Q\), bias, timing, or a wrong measurement model.

Current operational values

Present-stage legacy measurement covariance

\[R_{\mathrm{legacy}}=\operatorname{diag}\!\left( 1.21\times10^{-2},\ 3.6\times10^{-5},\ 2.25\times10^{-2},\ 2.25\times10^{-2},\ 1.0966\times10^{-2}\right).\]

The entries correspond to barometric height, downward range, optical-flow North/East velocity, and legacy magnetic heading. The last variance represents a conservative 6° heading sigma in radians.

Stationary measurement floors

These residual distributions establish lower bounds and expose quantization or non-Gaussian structure. They are useful starting evidence, not automatically safe flight covariances.

Rangefinder evidence across four flights

Quiet signal-domain estimates near 2 mm failed to generalize to the hardest flight. Complete-filter replay selected 6 mm as a defensible fixed value for the tested regime.

Multiplicative Extended Kalman Filter

Quaternion attitude with a small multiplicative error state

The nominal state carries position, velocity, quaternion attitude, accelerometer bias, gyroscope bias, and barometer bias. A 16-dimensional local error state is estimated and injected into the nominal state.

Nominal and error states

Keep the quaternion normalized without filtering four unconstrained components

\[x=\{p_N,v_N,q_{NB},b_a,b_g,b_h\},\] \[\delta x=[\delta p^T,\delta v^T,\delta\theta^T,\delta b_a^T,\delta b_g^T,\delta b_h]^T.\]

The quaternion belongs to the nominal state, while the filter estimates a three-component small-angle error \(\delta\theta\). After correction, that error is converted to a small quaternion and multiplied into the nominal attitude.

IMU prediction

Propagate navigation states and covariance

\[\dot p_N=v_N,\qquad \dot v_N=R(q_{NB})(a_m-b_a)+g_N,\] \[\dot q_{NB}=\tfrac12\Omega(\omega_m-b_g)q_{NB}.\]
\[P_k^-=F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k^T+G_kQ_{d,k}G_k^T.\]

Bias states follow random walks. Position uncertainty grows through the transition matrix even though white-noise injection enters velocity, attitude error, and bias blocks.

Measurement correction

Innovation, gain, and error-state injection

\[K_k=P_k^-H_k^TS_k^{-1},\qquad \delta\hat x_k=K_k\nu_k.\]

Position, velocity, biases, and barometer offset are corrected additively. Attitude is corrected multiplicatively:

\[q_{NB}^+=\delta q(\delta\hat\theta)\otimes q_{NB}^-.\]
Covariance correction

Joseph form and innovation gating

\[P_k^+=(I-K_kH_k)P_k^-(I-K_kH_k)^T+K_kR_kK_k^T.\]

The Joseph form protects covariance symmetry and positive semidefiniteness. A three-sigma scalar gate rejects a measurement when its normalized test ratio exceeds one; the equivalent scalar NIS threshold is nine.

ℹ

The optical-flow sign convention was verified experimentally. Reversing the body-velocity relation caused catastrophic horizontal divergence, illustrating why coordinate models must be tested before covariance tuning.

Conclusion

A covariance is a physical claim, not merely a tuning knob

The project combines sensor characterization and estimator design because the two cannot be defended separately. The final values are accepted only when their physical origin and filter-level consequences agree.

What was demonstrated

  • Allan, Hadamard, spectral, and time-domain tools diagnose different parts of the inertial-noise problem.
  • Measured IMU white noise can be translated into the MEKF process covariance without claiming that every slow bias term was identified.
  • Static measurement scatter is insufficient for dynamic flight; complete-filter replay is required.
  • A 6 mm range sigma produced statistically reasonable behavior on the difficult altitude-changing flight and conservative behavior on calmer flights.
  • NIS, rejection, covariance, trajectory shape, and estimator agreement must be interpreted together.

Remaining work

  • Complete a long temperature-aware IMU run to separate bias instability, drift, and random walk more reliably.
  • Identify optical-flow noise using an independent horizontal-velocity reference.
  • Replace the legacy scalar magnetic-heading update with a calibrated vector-field model and magnetometer-bias states.
  • Validate absolute trajectory accuracy using camera/PnP, motion capture, surveyed markers, or another independent reference.
  • Investigate adaptive measurement covariance when fixed \(R\) cannot remain consistent across all regimes.
