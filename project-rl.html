Custom PPO Environment for

A custom reinforcement-learning environment in which an agent coordinates two boxes at a time inside a structured workspace. The task combines motion decisions, spatial arrangement, and collision-aware behaviour, while the policy is trained with PPO and evaluated through training curves, architecture comparisons, and reward-surface analysis.

Overview

Project Overview

Objective

The agent observes the system configuration and selects actions that improve box alignment and separation while respecting the geometry of the workspace. The reward function encourages structured motion and penalises poor placement, overlap, and ineffective coordination.

The goal is to discover a policy \(\pi_\theta(a \mid s)\) that maximises the expected cumulative discounted return:

\[ J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{T} \gamma^t\, r_t \right] \]
ℹ

Here \(\gamma \in (0,1)\) is the discount factor and \(r_t\) is the reward at time step \(t\). The discount reduces the weight of future rewards, encouraging the policy to favour prompt, efficient coordination.

Why PPO

PPO was chosen because it offers stable policy updates through a clipped surrogate objective, works well with continuous and discrete control, and allows systematic tuning through entropy regularisation, clipping ratio, discounting, and network architecture choices.

\[ r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)} \] \[ \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[ \min\!\left(r_t(\theta)\,\hat{A}_t,\; \mathrm{clip}\!\left(r_t(\theta), 1{-}\varepsilon, 1{+}\varepsilon\right)\hat{A}_t \right) \right] \]
ℹ

\(\hat{A}_t\) is the advantage estimate at step \(t\) and \(\varepsilon\) is the clipping threshold (typically 0.1–0.2). The \(\min\) prevents the policy from benefiting from excessively large updates in either direction.

Environment

Environment and Task Setup

Custom Workspace Layout

The workspace is discretised into cells and used to model coordinated box handling. The environment includes spatial structure, directional motion, and conveyor-like behaviour, requiring the policy to learn both local action quality and global organisation simultaneously.

State Space

The state \(\mathbf{s}_t\) encodes the positions and configurations of the two boxes within the workspace grid at each time step. The agent observes this full state and selects a discrete action from the available motion set.

The state space dimensionality grows with the grid size, making generalisation across workspace configurations a non-trivial learning challenge.

Reward Design

The reward function \(r(\mathbf{s}_t, a_t)\) combines several terms:

  • Alignment bonus: positive reward for improving box separation and ordering.
  • Collision penalty: negative reward for invalid placements or overlapping configurations.
  • Progress term: shaped reward that guides the agent toward the target arrangement incrementally.
ℹ

Reward shaping is critical here — a sparse terminal reward alone makes the credit-assignment problem too difficult for PPO to solve reliably within a reasonable number of episodes.

Algorithm

PPO Training Algorithm

Full Objective

The combined loss that PPO minimises at each update step is:

\[ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta) - c_1\,\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{VF}}(\theta) + c_2\,\mathcal{H}\!\left[\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s_t)\right] \]

where \(\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{VF}}\) is the value-function squared-error loss and \(\mathcal{H}\) is the policy entropy. The coefficients \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) control the relative weight of each term.

Advantage Estimation

The advantage \(\hat{A}_t\) is estimated using Generalised Advantage Estimation (GAE), which balances bias and variance through a \(\lambda\) parameter:

\[ \delta_t = r_t + \gamma\,V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t) \] \[ \hat{A}_t = \sum_{k=0}^{T-t} (\gamma\lambda)^k\,\delta_{t+k} \]
ℹ

With \(\lambda = 0\) this reduces to the one-step TD error; with \(\lambda = 1\) it becomes the full Monte-Carlo return. Values between 0.9 and 0.97 are typically used in practice.

Training

Training Progress

Episode reward and moving-average reward during training for different entropy coefficient settings.

Entropy Coefficient \(c_2 = 0.03\)

This run shows a clear upward trend in the moving-average reward, indicating that the policy improves steadily over training. The expected episode-to-episode variability is present but the trend is consistent. Lower entropy encourages the policy to commit to actions earlier in training.

Entropy Coefficient \(c_2 = 0.05\)

Increasing the entropy coefficient encourages stronger exploration across the action space. The policy still converges but exhibits broader exploration during early training, which can help escape local optima in environments with deceptive reward structure.

Interpretation

The training curves and architecture comparison together show that the agent responds meaningfully to hyperparameter and network design choices. Higher-capacity networks achieve better final performance but require more episodes to converge. The reward-surface figure complements these results by revealing the underlying structure that the policy implicitly optimises over.

The entropy coefficient \(c_2\) acts as a direct control on the exploration–exploitation balance: lower values accelerate convergence at the cost of potentially settling in local optima, while higher values maintain diversity but slow convergence.

Analysis

Discussion

What the Results Show

The policy improves steadily through training and becomes increasingly capable of coordinating box motion according to the reward structure. The experiments also show that entropy tuning and network capacity affect both exploration breadth and final performance — two quantities that must be balanced against each other.

The reward-surface analysis confirms that the policy is not simply memorising a fixed sequence of actions but has learned to exploit the geometric structure of the reward landscape.

Why the Project Matters

This project connects reinforcement learning to a structured engineering task involving spatial reasoning, control decisions, and performance-driven reward design. It demonstrates how learning-based policies can be engineered, systematically evaluated, and improved through controlled experimentation with hyperparameters and architecture choices.

The framework — custom environment, shaped reward, PPO with entropy tuning, and architecture search — is directly transferable to more complex manipulation and logistics tasks in robotics.
