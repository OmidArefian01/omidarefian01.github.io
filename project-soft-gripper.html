Soft Silicone Gripper for Delicate Food Handling

A three-finger soft robotic gripper designed to adapt passively to fruits and other irregular objects. The prototype combines compliant silicone contact surfaces, a stiffer structural backbone, embedded 3D-printed flanges, and a compact single-motor tendon drive. The project covered concept development, simulation, mold design, multimaterial casting, assembly, Arduino control, and physical grasp testing.

Overview

Why a Soft Gripper?

Food-handling tasks combine uncertain object geometry with a narrow range of safe contact forces. The design shifts part of that complexity from active control into material compliance and geometry.

The Challenge

Variable Geometry and Bruising Risk

Fruits and other food products differ in size, curvature, surface texture, and stiffness. A rigid gripper must be positioned accurately and regulate force inside a narrow operating range: too much force can damage the object, while too little force can cause a failed grasp.

The Approach

Let the Structure Adapt

Soft fingers conform around an object without requiring every contact point to be commanded. The final concept uses three tendon-driven fingers around a central palm, allowing the compliant layers to absorb alignment errors while the stiffer layers guide the bending shape.

Mechanical Design

From Finger Geometry to Complete Gripper

The design was refined around controlled curvature, resistance to gravity, and a compact actuation mechanism that pulls all three fingers with one motor.

Finger

Guided Compliance

Unequal segment geometry and variable thickness guide the finger toward a useful curved grasp instead of allowing uncontrolled deformation.

Contact

Embedded Retention Flanges

Flexible side features increase the available contact envelope and help retain objects as the fingers close around different shapes.

Actuation

Single Pulley, Three Tendons

Three tendons pass through T-shaped guides and connect to one pulley. A standard servo winds the tendons together to produce a coordinated closing motion.

Final Finger Architecture

The compliant contact body, structural silicone layer, and PLA inserts work together to balance adaptability with repeatable bending.

Complete Gripper Assembly

Three fingers are arranged around the central palm and mounted above the servo-driven tendon mechanism.

Finger Deformation Study

Simulation was used to inspect the bending shape and identify where deformation concentrates along the segmented finger.

Assembly-Level Simulation

The assembled model was evaluated to check coordinated finger motion and the overall closing configuration before fabrication.

Material Strategy

Three-Layer Multimaterial Construction

Each material has a distinct mechanical role: conform to the object, constrain the bending path, or reinforce the finger and its retention features.

Contact Layer

Ecoflex 00-35

The most compliant layer forms the inner deformable body and adapts to the object surface during contact.

  • Mixing ratio1:1
  • Working time2 min
  • Cure time5 min
  • Volume42 ml
Backbone

Smooth-Sil 950

A stiffer silicone backbone constrains the deformation path and reduces uncontrolled collapse under tendon load.

  • Mixing ratio10:1
  • Working time45 min
  • Cure timeApprox. 18 h
  • Volume13 ml
Reinforcement

PLA Components

FDM-printed inserts support the flange geometry, add local stiffness, and connect the silicone structure to the rigid mechanism.

  • ProcessFDM printing
  • Finger inserts2 layers
  • Additional partsMolds and base

Material Roles in the Finger

The final stack combines a soft interactive layer, a structural backbone, and rigid embedded features rather than asking one material to provide every function.

Fabrication

Molding, Printing, and Assembly

The prototype was produced through an iterative workflow that connected finger geometry, negative mold design, sequential casting, rigid-part fabrication, and final integration.

01

Design

Define the external geometry and desired bending behaviour.

02

Create a three-part negative mold for the two silicone layers.

03

Cast

Pour and cure the elastomers while embedding the PLA components.

04

Print

Fabricate the base, inserts, mechanism, and mold components.

05

Assemble

Integrate the fingers, tendons, pulley, guides, and servomotor.

Three-Part Mold

Separate mold components define the Ecoflex body, position the PLA spine and flanges, and close the cavity for the Smooth-Sil layer.

Integrated Prototype

The completed assembly combines compliant fingers with the printed frame and compact tendon transmission in one portable unit.

Control

Simple Open-Loop Position Command

Material compliance performs much of the physical adaptation, allowing the first prototype to use a compact open-loop control architecture.

Hardware

Arduino and Servo Drive

  • An Arduino Nano R4 reads the command potentiometer.
  • A standard servomotor winds the common tendon pulley.
  • A LiPo battery supplies the portable electronics.
  • The command moves all three fingers together.
Software

Smoothed 50 Hz Command Pipeline

  • An eight-sample moving average reduces ADC noise.
  • The filtered 0-1023 value is mapped and constrained to 0°-180°.
  • The servo command is updated at 50 Hz.
  • Raw input and servo angle are streamed for live debugging.

Control Architecture

The goal pose is converted into a motor command without contact or force feedback in the current prototype.

Embedded Software Flow

Filtering, mapping, saturation, servo output, and serial diagnostics form the complete real-time loop.

Results and Testing

Grasping Food and Irregular Objects

Physical tests evaluated whether the shared tendon drive could close the three fingers around objects with different size and geometry while the soft layers conformed to contact.

Food-Handling Tests

The prototype was tested with an apple, carrot, and onions to examine its ability to approach and close around common food geometries.

Other Shapes

Additional objects were used to observe how the segmented fingers and flexible side features adapt outside the original food-handling scenario.

✓

End-to-End Prototype Completed

The project progressed from design and simulation to molds, multimaterial fingers, printed hardware, electronics, embedded control, and physical grasp demonstrations.

Future Development

Closing the Loop

The next iteration can combine the gripper's passive mechanical adaptation with sensing and feedback for more repeatable contact and grasp decisions.

Perception

Proximity Sensing

Detect the object before contact and use distance information to coordinate the closing command.

Feedback

Tactile Sensing

Measure contact distribution and stop or adjust tendon motion when the grasp becomes secure.

Retention

Suction Features

Explore passive or actively controlled suction elements for smoother and more delicate object surfaces.
