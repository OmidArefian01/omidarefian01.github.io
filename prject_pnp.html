KFPnP: Robust and Adaptive ArUco Pose Estimation

KFPnP rebuilds the ArUco-to-pose pipeline around a single idea: a pose solver is only as good as the confidence it places in each measurement. Classical marker detection treats every corner as equally trustworthy and every frame as equally lit — an assumption that breaks down under motion blur, glare, and low light. KFPnP replaces that assumption with a marker-recovery stage that stays robust when classical detection falters, and a live uncertainty model that tells the pose solver exactly how much to trust each corner, frame by frame.

Overview

Why a Fixed Noise Assumption Falls Short

Conventional PnP pipelines feed every detected corner into the solver with the same assumed measurement noise, no matter how the marker was actually lit or how sharply its corners were resolved. KFPnP was built to close that gap.

The Problem With "One Noise Value Fits All"

A pose solver that weights every corner equally is implicitly told that a corner seen in bright, sharp conditions is just as reliable as one glimpsed near the sensor's darkness limit or smeared by motion. In practice that forces the solver to either under-trust its best measurements or over-trust its worst ones — there is no way to tell them apart after the fact.

KFPnP addresses this at two points in the pipeline: recovering the marker's identity even when classical detection is uncertain, and estimating a fresh, per-corner measurement uncertainty on every frame, so the pose solver can weight each observation by how much it actually deserves to be trusted.

Robust Identification

Recovering Markers When Classical Detection Struggles

Dictionary-based ArUco decoding is fast and reliable in good conditions, but it is a hard pass/fail test: a blurred, tilted, or partially shadowed marker either decodes cleanly or is discarded entirely. KFPnP adds a learned fallback that degrades gracefully instead.

Training

Learning From Synthetic Variation

A classifier is trained on a large, synthetically generated set of marker crops that span the distortions a real camera actually produces — blur, rotation, partial occlusion, and lighting extremes — rather than only the clean, frontal examples a classical decoder assumes.

Inference

Identity, Orientation, Confidence

At runtime the classifier returns not just a marker ID but an orientation estimate and a confidence score for the prediction, so downstream stages know when to trust the recovered identity and when to fall back to classical redetection.

Outcome

Graceful Degradation

Instead of a marker vanishing from the tracked set the moment classical decoding fails, KFPnP keeps a confidence-scored identity available through exactly the conditions — blur, glare, partial visibility — where it matters most.

Marker Recovery Under Degradation

Raw camera footage of the markers being identified live — recovering identity, orientation, and confidence exactly where classical dictionary decoding alone would drop the detection entirely.

Adaptive Noise

A Per-Corner Uncertainty, Recomputed Every Frame

Instead of a single fixed pixel-noise constant, KFPnP estimates a fresh measurement uncertainty for every detected corner on every frame — built from two complementary signals that are combined into one covariance the pose solver can consume directly.

Signal 1

Sensor Noise, Calibrated Offline

A camera's own read noise and photon noise depend on how bright the scene is and how much gain the sensor is applying — a corner observed at low light and high gain is simply noisier than one observed in bright, low-gain conditions. This relationship is characterized once, offline, against the camera itself, and evaluated live using only the current frame's brightness and gain.

Signal 2

Local Image Structure

A well-defined corner with strong gradients in two directions constrains its own position tightly; a corner that looks more like an edge is poorly constrained along that edge's direction. KFPnP reads this directly from the local gradient structure around each corner, so two corners can receive different uncertainty estimates even under identical lighting.

Combined

One Covariance, Recomputed Live

The two signals are blended into a single per-corner covariance matrix, evaluated independently for every corner on every frame — never fitted once and reused, and never assumed to be the same across the frame.

Integration

Feeding Uncertainty Into the Pose Solver

The per-corner covariance is not a diagnostic on the side — it is consumed directly by the pose solver, so corners that are more reliable this frame are trusted more this frame.

Weighting

Reliability-Weighted Refinement

The camera pose is recovered through an iterative refinement that weights each corner's contribution by the inverse of its estimated uncertainty: a sharp, well-lit corner pulls the solution toward it more strongly than a dim, poorly constrained one, and that balance is free to shift from one frame to the next.

Output

Pose With Its Own Covariance

The solver's output is not just a position and orientation — it also carries a propagated six-degree-of-freedom pose covariance, giving any downstream filter or controller an honest measure of how much to trust that frame's estimate.

Uncertainty That Tracks Reality

As the scene was dimmed and re-brightened by hand, the reported per-corner and propagated pose uncertainty rose and fell in step — consistently across all three markers, regardless of their position or orientation on screen — confirming that the effect is driven by illumination itself, not by any single marker's geometry.

Conclusion

Conclusion

KFPnP shows that a pose pipeline improves the most not by chasing a better point estimate, but by being honest about how much to trust each measurement.

Key Findings

  • A learned identification stage keeps markers trackable through blur, glare, and partial visibility where classical decoding alone would drop them.
  • Per-corner uncertainty that reacts to both sensor conditions and local image structure predicts real measurement scatter far better than a fixed constant.
  • Feeding that uncertainty into the pose solver, rather than treating every corner equally, propagates cleanly through to pose covariance without any online re-fitting.
  • The effect was consistent across three independently tracked markers under live, hand-varied lighting — not an artifact of one marker's geometry.

Where This Fits

KFPnP is designed as a drop-in front end for any MLPnP-style solver that already accepts per-observation weights: swap in a robust identity, swap in a live covariance, and the rest of the pose-estimation and downstream filtering stack is unchanged.
